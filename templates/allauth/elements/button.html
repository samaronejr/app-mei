Still an anchor when it is given a destination and a button otherwise, exactly as upstream: a control that navigates must be a link, or it is unreachable to anyone driving the page from the keyboard by role, and it loses open-in-new-tab. `tags` is how allauth says how loud a control should be — the sign-in and sign-up submits arrive tagged prominent. That maps onto the primary ramp at the large size, full width, because an entrance card has exactly one thing to do and the control that does it should look like it. Everything else is quiet. The tag list is checked for truth before it is searched. `attrs.tags` is absent on most calls, and while Django's if-tag swallows the resulting TypeError and answers false, a guard that depends on an exception being caught is a guard nobody can read. `danger` is the third ramp, and it is a ramp rather than a decoration: the controls that carry it remove a second factor or invalidate a set of recovery codes, and the quiet ramp would make either look exactly like cancelling. The tag is checked AFTER `prominent` so a control that somehow arrived with both still reads as the affirmative action rather than a destructive one; nothing in the reachable tree passes both. Both branches spell their classes as literals.
