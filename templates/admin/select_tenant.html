What it did not give it was a page: the controls sat alone in a card that said nothing about what choosing does, on the one screen in this product where the honest answer is "you are about to read another firm's CNPJs, CPFs and audit stream". So the composition here is mostly copy, and every claim it makes is one the code already enforces. `record_selection` writes a TENANT_SELECTED event on every successful choice and a second, separate IMPERSONATION event carrying the stated reason when the firm is outside the operator's memberships. `may_operate_as` is re-run on every admin request rather than at selection time, which is what makes "perder o vínculo encerra o acesso na hora" true rather than aspirational. And `authorize` refuses a break-glass attempt with no reason outright. The refusal is rendered through the shared notice partial instead of the bare paragraph it used to be. Same ramp, but the partial is what emits the interrupting role, and the title is load-bearing beside it: the reasons `authorize` raises are written for an operator who already knows what they clicked — "This account has no active membership for that firm" arriving alone above a dropdown reads as a sentence about nothing. Two glyphs, both wayfinding rather than ornament. An operator is usually thrown to this screen mid-task by the middleware, and the first question is always which of the two things went wrong — the company or the account. The building marks the section that answers the first; the document marks the one that answers the second. Neither carries meaning on its own: the refusal is a colour, a role and a sentence. {% endcomment %}
