Which is why the notice is the first thing and says the two facts that decide whether the form gets filled in at all: no account is needed, and the answer goes to the address typed below. The second is not a courtesy — `request_received.html` promises a reply "para o e-mail informado no formulário", and a typo there is a request that can never be answered and cannot be corrected afterwards, because the view keeps no other channel back to the requester. The section under the form explains the only two controls a stranger hesitates on. The relationship field offers exactly two values in the model, holder and representative, and it is the field that decides who the answer may be handed to. The CPF is the one piece of data on this page a person is right to be careful with, so the page says what it is for and where it lands: `DataSubjectRequest` is deliberately NOT append-only — its own docstring says so — precisely so the number can be erased later, while the audit trail beside it never records a document at all. The glyph is on that section and nowhere else. It marks the part of the page that is about a PERSON rather than about the request, which is the distinction the two paragraphs under it turn on; the form's own controls are labelled and need no help. {% endcomment %}
