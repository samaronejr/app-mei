Every value on a row is a column of the row itself. Nothing here reaches through a foreign key, and the reason is not performance: rendering happens inside the portal transaction and under `app_portal`, a role holding SELECT on seven tables, and the account table is not one of them. Reaching for the person who sent the file is therefore not a slower page -- it is `permission denied` on a screen a client already had open, and it lands on the table where the password hashes live. The two other keys on this row point at tables the role CAN read, which makes reaching through those quieter and no better: a query per row, issued during rendering, inside the transaction. A guard test refuses all three by name. The date is written twice and only once for a human. The `datetime` attribute carries the stored instant with its offset, which is what a machine reads; the text beside it is the same instant in Brasília time, which is what the footer promises and what a person here actually works to. The size is stated because a name alone cannot tell a real document apart from a truncated upload -- the case where a phone lost signal halfway through and the client has no way to know.
